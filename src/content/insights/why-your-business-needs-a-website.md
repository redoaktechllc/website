Plenty of good local businesses run on a Facebook or Instagram page and nothing else. It's free, it's familiar, and it works — up to a point. The trouble is you're building on rented land.

## A social page isn't really yours

The platform owns the page, sets the rules, and decides who sees your posts. Reach changes without warning. Accounts get locked by mistake. And when someone searches for a plumber or a painter in your town, a buried Facebook page rarely shows up the way a real website does.

## A website is your home base

Think of social media as the sign on the road and your website as the shop it points to. The site is the one place you fully control. It works around the clock, it can show up in Google and Google Maps, and it's built to do exactly what you need:

- Answer the three questions every customer has — what you do, where, and how to reach you — in a few seconds on a phone.
- Put a tap-to-call button and a quote form right where people look.
- Show your real work, your hours, and the services you want to sell most.

## You don't need a big, expensive site

For most local businesses, one clear page does the job. It should load fast, look right on a phone first, and make it obvious how to get in touch. That's it. Pair it with a Google Business Profile and you're both easy to find and easy to trust.

Keep the social pages — they're great for staying in touch with regulars. Just don't let them be the only thing standing in for your business online.
